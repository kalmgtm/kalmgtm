The situations

Three things that
happen before the job post.

By the time a company posts a role on LinkedIn, your competitors already know about it. The system watches for what happens before the post goes up.

01
They just raised.

A company closes a Series A, B, C, or D. Funding announcements are public record. Within 30 days, the executive team starts hiring across sales, engineering, and marketing. The system reads the filing and reaches the VP of Sales before the recruiter job req is even written.

02
The role has been open 60 days.

Job postings are public. When a role sits open for two months, the internal recruiting team can't fill it. That company needs outside help and most of them know it. The system flags these daily and identifies the hiring manager, not the recruiter.

03
A new leader just started.

A new CRO or VP of Engineering starts at a company. Leadership changes are public. New leaders hire within their first 90 days, almost every time. The system catches the announcement and reaches them while they're still building their plan.

The real problem

You bought the tools.
They're not running.

Most staffing agencies under 20 people have the same story. Bought outbound software. Ran a few campaigns. Nothing stuck. The tools sit there.

The issue was never the software. The issue is that nobody set up the targeting, wrote the outreach, or built the logic that connects a funding round on Tuesday to an email in the right inbox on Wednesday.

That's what the pilot builds. If you already own the tools, even better. No new subscriptions. Just someone to make what you bought actually work.
